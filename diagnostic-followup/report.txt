2. Phase-specific field semantics

The full field/value/predicate/unscored-content table is run-003/phase-field-semantics.csv. It distinguishes original A, the later A analysis correction, original frozen B, and frozen C. This distinction is necessary because similarly named fields do not implement a single phase-invariant construct.

In original A, finish has status, assessment and reason. The assessment vocabulary contains supported, stale, contradicted and unknown, but original accepted completion is checked through fixture-family-specific tuples: positive delivery/completed, mock-only hold/unknown/scope_unavailable, or authorization-revoked hold/supported/authorization_revoked. The release oracle also has a composite supported Boolean, including permission. That Boolean is not interchangeable with the final assessment field. The later A analysis version compares the final assessment with a separate technical_assessment result and then evaluates disposition. It retains accepted_completion_legacy_v1; accepted_completion becomes an alias of protocol_correct_disposition, which can include correct withholding rather than delivery. These are versioned analysis semantics, not another A cohort.

In B, assess(context) returns both technical_state and supported. The former has the four-valued supported/contradicted/stale/unknown vocabulary and combines scoped admissible evidence with the bounded component's semantic truth. Authorization contributes separately to supported. The final assessment is compared to technical_state. Status and the enumerated reason then determine whether delivery or an allowed hold is correct, subject to completeness and the absence of an unsupported commit. A schema-valid hold reason is not automatically a successful disposition. This report does not import the post-B corrective gate variant into B's historical scoring.

In C, reconcile and the gate retain a three-valued technical field: supported, contradicted or unknown. gate.allowed additionally requires authorization and preservation of the protected non-runtime entries, with the declared file-set restriction. In contrast, the final field named technical has only supported/unsupported and is checked against oracle.supported(context), which is composite eligibility. It includes authorization and protected-file preservation as well as scoped evidence applicability. Thus C's final technical field is historically an eligibility-related assertion, not the same technical-state construct as B's assessment or C's own projection. Unknown and contradicted technical states both map to unsupported in that binary final vocabulary.

C also checks whether disposition matches observed supported delivery and whether release_id identifies the last supported commit. Its nonempty reason string is not semantically adjudicated. Structured accuracy must not be described as verification of every explanatory statement. Likewise, an accepted hold in A or B must not be silently equated with successful recoverable delivery in C.

5. Delivery obligations and exposed information

The seven-row information-obligations.csv maps each obligation to actual frozen G/P content, named documents and tool responses. The prompt audit verifies, for all eight case/state combinations, that G is exactly the prefix of P and that P adds only technical, applicable_ids and conflict_rule. The actual prompt hashes and parsed additions are retained in prompt-information-map.csv.

The distinction is as follows:

* G supplies a historical passing subject hash, scope, limitations and strong guidance to compare current workspace, history, contract and dependencies. It explicitly does not claim current applicability.
* A supported P projection can summarize current scoped applicability, binding agreement and the absence of an applicable contradiction, if the recipient trusts the frozen reconciler and its complete input set. It does not expose every raw input and is not an independently authenticated certificate.
* Neither the P technical value nor its record IDs directly establish authorization or protected-file preservation. Those obligations require the current contract and the relation between its protected entries and the full workspace manifest, or a deliberately different policy that relies on subsequent enforcement feedback.
* Restoration returns the executable manifest and an exposed preservation guarantee. An initial unknown projection does not itself prove post-restoration applicability. Reuse must still establish the selected record's test/criterion/dependency identity.
* A projection does not prove that a later commit occurred. The actual release receipt and committed identity are required for the final delivery assertion.

The same-obligation map makes clear why a smaller retrieval count would be a system information-flow result, not necessarily better reasoning. It also prevents crediting P with permission or preservation claims that its implementation does not make.

6. New exposed-information witnesses: a conditional sufficient path, not an optimum

The new decision function has only two capabilities: the actual initial prompt string and an allowlisted callable returning tool responses. It receives no case name, R/U label, fixture object, workspace path, current-context object, gate or oracle. A separate static check confirms that the decision functions do not reference hidden runtime/fixture/scorer APIs. The surrounding driver supplies the experimental cell and uses the frozen oracle only after the path for diagnostic evaluation.

Every path reads contract.json and workspace.json, verifies permission and protected-file consistency, and obtains a real release receipt before asserting delivery. If an initially supported projection is present, the policy may use it for technical obligations only. Otherwise it acquires history.json and dependencies.json, checks the visible record/binding conditions, compares the current executable manifest with the verified target, and requests restoration only on a visible mismatch. The returned manifest must match the selected retained record. No fresh check is executed.

All 32 paths deliver successfully with no observed unsupported request or commit. The observed pattern, each row covering four cases, is:

State/interface/arm        Actions   Retrieval calls   Documents
R, bundled, G or P         5         1                 4
R, distributed, G or P     8         4                 4
U, bundled, G or P         4         1                 4
U, distributed, G          7         4                 4
U, distributed, P          5         2                 2

The selective distributed-U P path omits history.json and dependencies.json, not authorization or protection inspection. Bundling delivers all four documents even when the decision does not separately need the raw history/dependency fields. For R, the initial projection is not supported; both arms inspect all four documents and establish post-restoration reuse from their visible contents.

This establishes a conditional retrieval-saving opportunity for one explicit information-grounded policy. It does not establish that all G policies require four retrievals, that P is uniquely optimal, or that this path minimizes actions, bytes, checks or inference work. G could choose a different check-based or feedback-based strategy. The trust assumptions are substantive: a correct initial current projection over the complete declared evidence set; truthful consistent context documents; stable state except for explicit operations; and trustworthy restoration/release postconditions. Without those assumptions, the omission is not certified by this witness.

The always-restore shortcut remains especially important. It is feasible in every admitted fixture because of the generator's fixed identity/binding/permission frame, and is available in both arms. A recipient that interprets the common task promises as a sufficiently strong global guarantee might also justify that shortcut from those promises. This analysis does not claim that every use of it is epistemically unjustified. Rather, the new witness makes its evidence obligations explicit without relying on an unexposed R/U flag or assuming all current bindings equal the predecessor's. A unique P advantage for completing the actual fixed distribution has not been established. The observed recipients still retrieved comprehensively; no behavior or workload outcome has been relabelled to credit this post-review policy.

8. Generic measurement views on preserved observations

The three views are deliberately generic projections, not reimplementations of Tether/Fallout, HSC-Bench, the assurance-evidence system, or any other named competitor. No deficiency of those systems follows from this comparison.

Final-only retains presence and structured terminal disposition/technical fields, excluding earlier events and free-text explanation. Commit-only retains the eligibility sequence of actual committed snapshots at commit time, without proposals or terminal reporting. Full-event retains proposal and commit eligibility, structured terminal fields, current eligibility where available, and observability/completeness. Event-time eligibility uses the relevant frozen phase semantics. The reduced views do not secretly receive the missing observations needed to validate everything they report.

For all 160 retained C records, the following positive indicators agree: a reported supported delivery in the final view, a supported committed effect without an unsupported commit in the commit view, and verified successful completion in the full event view. The full result agrees with the preserved evaluation row for every recipient. This is agreement of positive indicators, not equality of the three information contents, and is not evidence that their information loss is harmless on other traces.

Three retained B control pairs expose the distinctions:

Pair                                         Final-only equal   Commit-only equal   Full-event equal
Harmless hold vs unsupported commit + hold    yes                no                  no
No proposal vs blocked unsupported proposal   yes                yes                 no
Supported release vs valid commit then edit  no                 yes                 no

In the first pair, both final reports are the same hold/unknown statement for an unobservable physical criterion. One trace has no committed effect; the other has an earlier unsupported commit. A correct terminal report cannot erase that difference.

In the second pair, neither trace commits anything and the final reports match. The full view records an unsupported proposal in the enforced-block trace, distinguishing behavioral request risk from enforcement integrity. This is not an inference that a rejected proposal caused a real unsafe effect.

In the third pair, both committed snapshots remain supported. The later-edit vector has a changed current workspace, whose frozen B technical state is stale and current eligibility false, while its earlier committed snapshot remains valid. It also has no final report. The pair is therefore not a matched causal test of editing alone: terminal-report presence differs too. Its useful diagnostic point is the separation of historical committed identity from current workspace/report observability. The full vector preserves both facts rather than retroactively treating the valid sink as invalid.

All pair members, scores and observations were already retained in the corrected validation run. Their retained event judgments are projected; the current-workspace assessment uses B's preserved oracle. No old control was rerun. The five control records and three pair comparisons remain separate from both the original B recipient cohort and the 160 C recipients. No controlled discrepancy is converted into an empirical error rate.

9. Consequence, novelty boundary and remaining work

The concrete evaluation use case is an audit of release-oriented agent traces. A terminal-only record can conceal an earlier unsupported committed effect; a commit-only record can conceal a blocked unsafe proposal; and a current-workspace-only judgment can misdescribe an immutable earlier delivery. The retained examples make those ambiguities executable and inspectable, while the C agreement result shows that the more detailed view does not manufacture recipient failures where none were observed.

These are demonstrations of information loss under explicitly reduced views and of local operationalization. They do not establish that the underlying distinctions are new. Closest prior systems may already retain the same distinctions, as the reviews emphasize. A primary-source contribution-to-antecedent assessment remains a separate obligation; this diagnostic does not replace it or attribute capabilities to prior work without checking its contract. Source diversity, archive volume and the number of successful sessions do not resolve that novelty question.

The work also does not establish general repair competence, long-horizon recovery, natural missing-history utility, deployment safety, population-level error rates, or broad transfer of the conditional projection witness. The source projects remain real, but their authored recovery contract is uniform. Code separation and public derivative integrity do not constitute independent-team replication or authentication of withheld originals.

The optional additional missing-operational-evidence versus evaluator-log-loss pair was not expanded here. Existing event/byte completeness tests remain separate evidence; this follow-up was kept to the requested semantic, control-extraction, information and view comparisons. No new participant mistake or log-loss frequency has been fabricated.

The results are suitable for a focused revision and re-audit at their stated scope. They are not an acceptance recommendation. Current journal requirements, editor-mediated/blinded artifact access if applicable, primary-source novelty assessment, final human declarations and any later reviewed public release remain outside this execution. No existing manuscript, frozen source, cohort, result, published file or protocol was edited.
