## Findings and required resolutions

1. **Information flow, not mere availability.** P may automatically acquire evidence and expose its decision-relevant consequences before participant retrieval. A smaller access effect for P can therefore mean automation substitutes for participant acquisition, not better participant reasoning. Freeze a table of raw records, initial content, computed facts, computation inputs, remaining retrieval, and intervention location. Require byte identity within each arm across access regimes, not across intentionally different G/P representations.
2. **Mechanical overhead versus behaviour.** Bundling necessarily saves requests for a policy that retrieves all documents; distributed retrieval may save bytes for selective policies. Report reference-policy overhead, actual participant acquisition, and decision outcomes separately. A reference policy is not a proved optimum. Verify delivery is not truncated.
3. **Opportunity is not established by a scripted path alone.** The reference may know repair locations or expected outcomes. Disclose such knowledge, define how errors/listings/retries consume the cap, allow recovery, and report cap-limited results under the chosen envelope. Do not increase budgets selectively after observing arm outcomes.
4. **Rollback and construct.** Prefer the narrow evidence-applicability/acquisition/release study with rollback permitted if legitimate. Do not make the task artificially difficult to escape the ceiling. Record whether restoring the verified snapshot satisfies each contract. If true universally, disclose it; diverse source projects do not create diverse repair strategies.
5. **Documentation changes.** Functional irrelevance does not preserve a whole-repository hash. Define scoped executable identity and the relation to the full delivered snapshot before assigning reuse expectations. Test the implemented rule rather than relying on a natural-language irrelevance label.
6. **Outcomes and denominators.** Distinguish supported completion, unsupported requests, unsupported commits, justified hold, missed recoverable opportunity, and operational/budget incompleteness. Define recoverability before treatment. Keep unlaunched assignments in the flow ledger, not as observed safe behaviour. An interrupted session can establish an observed unsafe event without an observed final disposition.
7. **Enforcement integrity.** With the same enforced gate in G/P, unsafe commits principally test gate/integration integrity. Unsupported requests before feedback are the behavioural safety outcome. Post-feedback recovery is behaviour within an enforced system.
8. **Selection and stopping.** Freeze candidate-search bounds/order, eligibility/exclusion reasons, structural coverage, admitted cases, fixture-defect handling, balanced scheduling, and stopping. Neutral transport preflight should not leak scientific-case performance into selection. No replacing cases because of success, holds, or small contrasts.

## Reduced design recommendation

Use G and P, bundled and distributed access, two distinct interruption states per case, and five fresh-session/order repetitions. Four structurally distinct cases from at least three projects give 160 scientific assignments. This is a feasibility/coverage recommendation, not a statistical threshold or power calculation. Eight cases would give 320 only if their structural coverage justified the extra work before collection. The original 640 sessions are not justified by the stated principal question; B0/B1 add estimands already outside that core.

A 10,800-second ceiling and two occupied slots imply at most 33.75 seconds average per assignment for 640 assignments before overhead. This is a capacity calculation, not measured runtime. Balanced blocks and partial-block accounting are necessary.

For outcome Y, define distributed-minus-bundled contrasts within G and P and their interaction I = (Y_PD − Y_PB) − (Y_GD − Y_GB). Report case/state contrasts and a prespecified finite-suite aggregation. Repetition indices pair schedules, not provider randomness.

## Resource ledger

Distinguish logical reads, physical I/O, model-visible tokens, cumulative bytes, and unique substantive bytes.
